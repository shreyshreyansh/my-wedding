# Story research: two homes, two faiths

**For:** the invitation site of Shreyansh (groom; family from Bihar, living in Ranchi; Hindu) and Mrunalini (bride; Pune, Marathi; Buddhist). Wedding 8–9 Dec 2026, Ranchi.
**Researched:** 3 Oct 2026. Builds on `docs/01-research.md`, which was written before the bride's family's Buddhism was known. Some of its suggestions need revisiting because of that (see §e).

## How to read this

**Source grades:**
- **[A]** Academic, museum, encyclopaedia, UNESCO, or the primary text itself (SuttaCentral, Cowell's Jataka).
- **[B]** Wikipedia, reputable newspapers, PARI, community publications, book excerpts.
- **[C]** Weak: commercial wedding blogs, panchang sites, enthusiast wikis. Never use a [C] claim on the site without an elder confirming it.

"(snippet)" means only a search summary or abstract was seen, not the full text.

**Confidence for bridges:**
- **High:** a primary or authoritative source says it directly.
- **Medium:** true, but a traditional identification, a thematic parallel, or contested.
- **Low:** plausible, not verified.

**Mark used in captions:** ⚑ = an elder or the couple must confirm before it goes on the site.

### Five things to know before anything else
1. **Ambedkar's 22 vows name Ganapati.** Vow 3 reads: "I shall have no faith in Gauri, Ganapati and other gods and goddesses of Hindus, and I shall not worship them." The same vows reject Brahma, Vishnu, Mahesh, Rama and Krishna (1–2), "the Buddha as an incarnation of Vishnu" (5), and "ceremonies performed by Brahmins" (8). They are carved at Deekshabhoomi and taken at conversion ([Wikipedia, with Omvedt 2003 and Indian Express 2022 cited] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-two_vows_of_Ambedkar)). **An opening that is only "॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥" over a Ganesha painting therefore speaks for his family, not hers.** Section (c) gives options.
2. **Practice in Buddhist families varies, so ask; don't assume.** Beltz (OUP) found that "many old traditions and rituals that could be labelled 'Hindu' continued to exist" among Maharashtra's Buddhists, while "many Buddhists criticize" that. He also argues against calling them syncretic ([Beltz, *Contesting Caste, Hierarchy, and Hinduism*] [A](https://bibliographie.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/138249/Beltz_032.pdf?sequence=1)).
3. **Dates.**
   - **6 Dec is Mahaparinirvan Din,** Ambedkar's death anniversary, a major day for Ambedkarite Buddhists ([Beltz] [A](https://bibliographie.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/138249/Beltz_032.pdf?sequence=1)). It falls two days before the Haldi.
   - **8 Dec 2026 is Amavasya,** the new moon: about 04:12 IST on 8 Dec to 06:21 IST on 9 Dec. That is Drik Panchang's times converted from US Eastern ([C](https://www.drikpanchang.com/vrats/amavasyadates.html?year=2026)). Both the Haldi and the Sangeet fall on it.
   - **9 Dec opens the bright half of Agahan (Margashirsha).** Jassal recorded that in the Bhojpuri region Agahan, "popularly believed to be the month in which Sita was married, is considered inauspicious for weddings" ([Jassal, Duke UP 2012] [A](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/45663)).
   - None of this is ours to decide: the purohit and the elders have almost certainly looked already. **Just don't put a muhurat or tithi line on the site unless the purohit supplies it, and avoid Ram–Sita "ideal couple" imagery** (see §e).
4. **All events are in Ranchi.** Several Bihari rites traditionally happen at the *bride's* home: parichhan, the kohbar, chaturthi. Ask how the families are adapting them before writing about them.
5. **Don't name caste,** or use "Mahar", "Dalit", "convert" or "neo-Buddhist" anywhere on the site. Use the words the family uses for themselves (likely "Buddhist" or "Bauddha"). The census, conversion numbers and politics stay out of captions.

## (a) Customs, chapter by chapter, for each side

### a1. His side: Bihari Hindu (Ranchi, roots in Bihar)

Ranchi was in Bihar until Jharkhand was "carved out of the… southern part of Bihar" in 2000 ([Wikipedia: Jharkhand] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharkhand)). **Ask the family which region of Bihar they come from (Mithila, Bhojpur or Magadh):** the customs below differ by region.

| Chapter | Custom | What it is | Bihari, or pan-Indian? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before | **Tilak** | The betrothal. The bride's father or brother goes to the groom's home and puts a tilak on his forehead, formally accepting him, with gifts. Jassal glosses Tilak as "betrothals". | North Indian. Strongest in Bihar, Jharkhand and eastern UP. | [Jassal] [A](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/45663); [Aza Fashions] [C](https://www.azafashions.com/blog/tilak-ceremony-bihar-jharkhand-wedding-meaning-significance-2026/) |
| Before | **Matkor** (Sanskrit *mṛdāharaṇa*) | Women of the family go to a pond, river or field and dig sacred earth while singing. The earth is used for the wedding hearth and altar. *Mati* (earth) + *kor* (dig). | Bihar, Jharkhand and eastern UP (a Bhojpuri word). | [Aza Fashions] [C](https://www.azafashions.com/blog/bihar-jharkhand-wedding-traditions-rituals-guide-2026/). ⚑ Only weak sources found; have an elder describe their version. |
| Haldi | **Haldi** | The bride's female kin anoint her with turmeric and herbal oils while women sing. | Pan-Indian. | [Jassal] [A](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/45663) (fieldwork in Jaunpur, a Bhojpuri district of eastern UP, not Bihar) |
| Haldi / wedding | **Chumavan** (चुमावन) | Pairs of married women touch the bride's feet, knees, shoulders and forehead with dub grass dipped in turmeric and milk, then touch their own lips, while the others sing blessings. Wikipedia describes rice coloured with turmeric, dub grass and curd, with five parts of the body touched, to *chumavan geet*. In Mithila, elders also chant the Durwakshat mantra. | Mithila and Bhojpur. | [Jassal, fieldnotes 2002] [A](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/45663); [Wikipedia: Chumavan] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumavan); [Wikipedia: Maithil Vivah] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithil_Vivah) |
| Haldi | **Geet-gawai** | The Bhojpuri women's pre-wedding song ceremony. Five married women tie turmeric, rice, grass and money in a cloth, the dholak is honoured, and songs and dancing follow. UNESCO inscribed it in 2016 for Mauritius's Bhojpuri community, which traces its origins to Bihar and UP. | Bhojpuri (and its diaspora). | [UNESCO ICH 01178] [A](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01178) |
| Music | **Vivah geet / mangal geet** | Women name their wedding songs after the rite each accompanies. In the Bhojpuri belt, women's auspicious *mangal* songs herald the baraat. | Bihar and the wider north. | [Mishra, IGNCA *Vihangama* 2001] [A](https://ignca.gov.in/PDF_data/Classification_Structure_Maithili_FolkSongs.pdf); [Jassal] [A](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/45663) |
| Music | **Gali** (गाली) | Teasing, often bawdy songs aimed at the other side's men, "integral to wedding celebrations". They restore balance between the bride's and groom's families, and reassure the bride. | North Indian (UP, Bihar and the Bhojpuri diaspora). Not uniquely Bihari. | [Jassal, ch. 3] [A](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/45663) |
| Music | **Sohar** | Birth songs. In Mithila they are also sung at the sacred-thread ceremony, the first haircut and marriage. | North Indian. **Not mainly a wedding genre**, so don't label sangeet songs "sohar". | [Mishra, IGNCA] [A](https://ignca.gov.in/PDF_data/Classification_Structure_Maithili_FolkSongs.pdf) |
| Music | **Domkach** | After the baraat leaves, the women of the groom's household stay up dancing in a linked semicircle, singing satirical, joyful songs. | Bihar (Mithila, Bhojpur, Magadh) and Jharkhand, where it is a Nagpuri form. | [Wikipedia: Domkach] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domkach) |
| Music | **Jhumair / jhumar** | The folk dance of Chotanagpur, with a Nagpuri jhumar, "mainly associated with the harvest season". | **Ranchi's own**: Jharkhand. | [Wikipedia: Jhumair] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhumair) |
| Wedding | **Parichhan** | The groom's welcome at the bride's door. Women ask him homely questions and sing *parichhan geet*. | Maithil. | [Wikipedia: Maithil Vivah] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithil_Vivah) |
| Wedding | **Aam-Mahu vivah** | Before the groom's party arrives, a mango tree is "married" to a mahua tree. | Maithil. | [Wikipedia: Maithil Vivah] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithil_Vivah) |
| Wedding | **Kanyadaan** | The father gives the bride. | Pan-Hindu. | Card programme in `docs/01-research.md` §10 |
| Wedding | **Sindoor daan** | The groom puts vermilion in the bride's hair parting: "the core spiritual union". In one Maithil account the groom first touches the kohbar painting with vermilion, then the bride. | North and East Indian, but **in Bihar it is *the* moment** of the wedding. | [Wikipedia: Maithil Vivah] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithil_Vivah); [ThePrint excerpt, *Mithila Art: A 360 Degree Review*] [B](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/maithil-weddings-arent-fixed-using-horoscope-a-phallic-kohbar-painting-is-more-important/2273044/) |
| Wedding | **Sinhora** (सिन्होरा) | The vermilion box ("sinhora" in Bhojpuri, Maithili and Magahi). A wooden sinhora is used in the marital home. Silver *sindoor daanis* are family heirlooms, given at bidaai. | Eastern India. | [Museum of Material Memory] [B](https://museumofmaterialmemory.com/?p=3208). ⚑ Who brings it, and when, varies. |
| Wedding | **Kohbar ghar** | The room in the bride's house where the family deity is installed and the rites are done. Its walls are painted (Madhubani) and the floor carries *aripan*. The groom must pick out his bride while she is hidden (*naina-jogin*). Motifs: lotus (female fertility), bamboo (a "multiplying lineage"), fish, and "parrots and peacocks… in pairs [for] union". | Mithila, Bihar, Jharkhand (*Khovar* in Hazaribagh). | [Wikipedia: Maithil Vivah] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithil_Vivah); [ThePrint excerpt] [B](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/maithil-weddings-arent-fixed-using-horoscope-a-phallic-kohbar-painting-is-more-important/2273044/). **Nuptial-chamber art with explicit symbolism: keep it discreet.** |
| Wedding | **Gauri puja** | The Maithil bride's thanksgiving to Gauri, painted in kohbar murals. | Maithil (and widely Hindu). | [UNC thesis, *Transforming the bride*] [A, snippet](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/record/uuid:22c2a2bc-41ae-4bf7-a7e3-ce7942f61288) |
| After | **Chaturthi, shagun, dwiragaman** | A Maithil marriage "is only considered complete after the Chaturthi (fourth-day) rites". *Shagun* gifts come the next morning. *Samdaun geet* are sung at the bride's departure. | Maithil. | [Wikipedia: Maithil Vivah] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maithil_Vivah) |

**Not wedding forms (avoid in sangeet copy):**
- **Jhijhiya:** Mithila's Navaratri/Dussehra dance for Durga, danced with lamp-lit pierced pots as protection against witchcraft ([Wikipedia: Jhijhiya] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhijhiya)).
- **Jat-jatin:** a monsoon duet about a couple's *separation*, also a drought ritual ([Wikipedia: Jatjatin dance] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jatjatin_Dance)).

**Sharda Sinha (1952–2024),** the "Bihar Kokila":
- She sang mainly in Maithili and Bhojpuri: vivah geet and Chhath geet, plus "Babul" for *Hum Aapke Hain Koun..!*.
- Padma Shri 1991, Padma Bhushan 2018, Padma Vibhushan 2025 ([Wikipedia] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharda_Sinha)).
- Mention her **as a reference only**. Her recordings are licensed (`docs/01-research.md` §10).

**Music of the region:**
- **Lavani:** song and dance to the *dholki*. Its songs are "usually naughty and erotic", though there is also a philosophical *nirguni* lavani ([Wikipedia: Lavani] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavani)). ⚑ Choose the number with the elders in mind.
- **Dhol-tasha:** the drum troupes of Pune's Ganesh processions, tied to Tilak's Ganeshotsav ([Outlook Traveller] [B](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/amp/story/destinations/india/echoes-of-bappa-how-ganesh-chaturthi-in-maharashtra-resounds-with-music-and-faith)). Fine for a baraat. Know that its association is with Ganesha.
- **Women's grindmill ovi:**
  - Over 110,000 songs were collected from 1,107 villages; the PARI Grindmill Songs Project holds them.
  - Wedding ovi invite Khandoba, Ambabai and "Ganaraya" to the family wedding ([PARI 2021] [B](https://ruralindiaonline.org/en/articles/i-invite-you-ganaraya-for-a-family-wedding/)).

### a3. Her family: Marathi Buddhist (Ambedkarite / Navayana) weddings

**Background, not for captions:**
- Marathi Buddhists are about 77% of India's Buddhists, and almost all follow the Navayana tradition ([Wikipedia: Marathi Buddhists] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_Buddhists)).
- Ambedkar took the three refuges and five precepts at Nagpur on **14 Oct 1956**, then gave them and the 22 vows to his followers ([Wikipedia: Deekshabhoomi] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deekshabhoomi); [Pritchett, Columbia] [A](https://franpritchett.com/00ambedkar/timeline/1950s.html)).
- Sources give 400,000 to 600,000 people for that day, so keep numbers off the site.

**Ritual pattern (Beltz, OUP) [A](https://bibliographie.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/138249/Beltz_032.pdf?sequence=1):**
- Maharashtrian Buddhists "adopted the ritual patterns of southern Buddhism as practised in Sri Lanka": Pali suttas, *buddha puja* and *buddha vandana*.
- Ambedkar compiled a handbook of Buddhist rituals.
- "There is a specific Buddhist way of marrying." At weddings "different texts are recited, the symbolic setting of statues, pictures, or flags is explicitly Buddhist".
- Hymns, candles, flowers and incense are pan-Indian; what differs is the understanding.

**How a ceremony usually runs.** As described by Mahendra Kamble, a Pune Bauddhacharya who has conducted 150+ weddings ([ThePrint, Ravikiran Shinde, 10 Sep 2023] [B](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/made-in-heaven-made-india-curious-about-dalit-buddhist-weddings-this-is-how-they-happen/1754900/)):
1. Candles are lit before photos of **Dr B. R. Ambedkar and the Buddha** (sometimes a copy of the Constitution too).
2. **Trisharan and Panchsheel:** the three refuges (*Buddhaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi…*) and the five precepts (no killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech or intoxicants).
3. The couple hold the ends of a **white cotton thread** laid in a **pot of water** between them.
4. **Buddha puja** and **Bhimsmaran**, remembering Ambedkar.
5. **Paritran path**, the protective chants.
6. **Mahamangala Sutta** ([Sn 2.4] [A](https://suttacentral.net/snp2.4/en/sujato)).
7. **The couple's vows** to respect each other.
8. **Jayamangala Atthagatha**, the "eight verses of joyous victory" ([translation] [B](https://aimwell.org/jayamangala.html); its role in modern Buddhist weddings: [Deegalle 2002] [A, snippet](https://researchspace.bathspa.ac.uk/2847)).
9. **Garlands exchanged amid flower petals** and applause.
10. The reception, in colourful clothes.

**Who officiates:**
- **Bauddhacharyas** (Bharatiya Bauddha Mahasabha), **Dhammacharis** (ThePrint names the Trailokya Bauddha Mahasabha) or **bhantes** (monks).
- "Any virtuous person can conduct the marriage ceremony" (Kamble, in ThePrint).

**Clothes and symbols:**
- **White** for bride and groom. Ambedkar's 1956 letter recommended "an earthen pot, a shared cotton thread, the recitation of the Mangala Sutta, and white clothing for both bride and groom" ([Round Table India, Suna] [B](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/selective-ritual-reform-and-buddhist-marriage-among-ambedkarites-in-odisha/); the article is about Odisha). ⚑ The letter itself was not traced.
- The **five-colour Buddhist ("Panchsheel") flag** was designed in Colombo in 1885 ([C](https://theosophy.wiki/en/Buddhist_Flag)).
- Greetings: **"Jai Bhim"** ([Wikipedia] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Bhim)) and "Namo Buddhaya".

**What is usually left out:**
- No muhurat, panchang or horoscope; often a weekend.
- **No havan, no saptapadi, no kanyadaan** ([ThePrint] [B](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/made-in-heaven-made-india-curious-about-dalit-buddhist-weddings-this-is-how-they-happen/1754900/)).
- **Sindoor and mangalsutra** are "frequently criticised as visible symbols of Brahmanical patriarchy", and many Ambedkarites reject them ([Round Table India] [B](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/selective-ritual-reform-and-buddhist-marriage-among-ambedkarites-in-odisha/)).
- No Brahmin priest (vow 8).

**What often continues:**
- The engagement, and the **baraat with music and dance** ([Round Table India] [B](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/selective-ritual-reform-and-buddhist-marriage-among-ambedkarites-in-odisha/), Odisha).
- **Bhim geet**, songs of love for Ambedkar, "played… [at] personal celebrations like birthdays, marriages, and home inaugurations" ([Round Table India, Vaibhav Kharat, 2023] [B](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/?p=137244); [Wikipedia: Dalit music] [B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit_music)). Singers include Prahlad Shinde, Anand Shinde and Kadubai Kharat.
- **Haldi:** no source found that documents it specifically for Marathi Buddhist weddings. ⚑ Very likely, but ask.
- **Mangalashtak-style verses:**
  - The Pali Jayamangala Atthagatha fills that slot.
  - Marathi relatives have written their own mangalashtaks for a century.
  - ⚑ Ask whether her family's Bauddhacharya uses Marathi Buddhist verses, and with what refrain.

**A precedent:** Mira and Yashwant Ambedkar (Babasaheb's son) married "बौद्ध पद्धतीने" (*in the Buddhist manner*) on 19 Apr 1953 at R. M. Bhat High School hall, Parel ([mr.wikipedia: मीरा आंबेडकर] [B](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/मीरा_आंबेडकर)).

**The Pali texts most likely to be heard, verified on SuttaCentral:**
- *Mahamaṅgala Sutta* (Sn 2.4), set at Sāvatthī:
  - "Mātāpitu upaṭṭhānaṁ, puttadārassa saṅgaho… etaṁ maṅgalamuttamaṁ" (*Caring for mother and father, kindness to children and partners… this is the highest blessing*).
  - "Dānañca dhammacariyā ca, ñātakānañca saṅgaho" (*Giving and righteous conduct, kindness to relatives*) ([A](https://suttacentral.net/snp2.4/en/sujato)).
- *Karaṇīya Metta Sutta* (Sn 1.8): "Sabbasattā bhavantu sukhitattā" (*May all beings be happy*) ([A](https://suttacentral.net/snp1.8/en/sujato)).
- *Siṅgāla Sutta* (DN 31), given **near Rājagaha (Rajgir, Bihar)**: a husband honours his wife, doesn't look down on her, is faithful, "relinquish[es] authority to her" and gives her adornments; she in turn is well organised, faithful and careful with their earnings ([A](https://suttacentral.net/dn31/en/sujato)). ⚑ Whether her Bauddhacharya uses DN 31 for vows is unverified.

**Legal note (off the site).** The Hindu Marriage Act 1955, s. 2, covers Buddhists ([bare Act text] [B](https://devgan.in/hma/chapter_01.php)). Many Ambedkarites object to this, and a separate Buddhist marriage law has been debated. So call the event "the wedding" or "Shaadi", never "a Hindu wedding".

## (c) The opening blessing: options that honour both faiths

**Principle:** give each family its own invocation at equal weight, plus one blessing both can say. **Don't merge the Buddha into a Hindu frame:**
- no composite "Ganesha + Buddha" shrine;
- no Dashavatara imagery (the Buddha as Vishnu's avatar is explicitly rejected in vow 5). The site's current Ganesha comes from a *Gita Govinda* series, whose opening hymn lists the avatars. The Indra-and-Ganesha folio itself is fine, but never use that series' avatar folio.

### Option A: two doorways, side by side (recommended as the base)
| | His family | Her family |
|---|---|---|
| Devanagari | ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥ | ॥ नमो बुद्धाय ॥ |
| Romanised | *Śrī Gaṇeśāya namaḥ* (Sanskrit) | *Namo Buddhāya* (Pali/Sanskrit) |
| English | Salutation to Lord Ganesha. | Homage to the Buddha. |
| Note | Standard opening of Hindu cards in both regions (`docs/01-research.md` §10). | A common Buddhist salutation in Maharashtra. Only weak sources found, e.g. a news headline quoting a minister's "Namo buddhaya" at a Buddha memorial in Nashik ([C](https://www.ap7am.com/en/89906/whatsapp:/send)). ⚑ Ask whether her family uses it on cards, or prefers the full homage below, or "Jai Bhim" ([B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Bhim)). |

**The full Pali homage**, chanted three times to open every Buddhist rite:
- Devanagari: **नमो तस्स भगवतो अरहतो सम्मासम्बुद्धस्स**
- Pali: *Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa*
- English: "Homage to the Blessed One, the Worthy One, the Rightly Self-awakened One" ([dhammatalks.org *Chanting Guide*] [A](https://dhammatalks.org/books/ChantingGuide/Section0004.html)).

### Option B: one shared word, *mangal* (recommended as the blessing line under A)
| | Text | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marathi / Sanskrit | **॥ शुभमंगल सावधान ॥** | "An auspicious moment: be attentive." The refrain called after every mangalashtak verse; the site's antarpat already uses it. | [mr.wikipedia] [B](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/मंगलाष्टक); `docs/01-research.md` §10 |
| Pali | **॥ भवतु सब्ब मङ्गलं ॥** (often printed भवतु सब्ब मंगलं) · *Bhavatu sabba-maṅgalaṁ* | "May there be every good blessing." The first line of a closing blessing: *Bhavatu sabba-maṅgalaṁ, rakkhantu sabba-devatā, sabba-buddhānubhāvena sadā sotthī bhavantu te*, i.e. "May there be every good blessing, may all the devas protect you; through the power of all the Buddhas [Dhamma, Saṅgha] may you always be well." | [dhammatalks.org *Chanting Guide* §IX] [A](https://dhammatalks.org/books/ChantingGuide/Section0085.html) |
| English | *"May every blessing be yours."* | Both lines say this. | |

**Verified meaning and use of "Bhavatu sabba mangalam":**
- It is a blessing for all good fortune, chanted at the *close* of Theravada blessing chants. The source is a Theravada chanting guide, with all three repetitions.
- As a standalone line it is safe and well known.
- **Don't add "rakkhantu sabba-devatā" (may all the gods protect you) unless her family uses it.** Some Ambedkarites may prefer to leave out the devas.

### Option C: "may all be happy" in two classical languages (faith-light)
- Sanskrit: **सर्वे भवन्तु सुखिनः** · *sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ* · "May all be happy." A traditional prayer, used as the NHRC's motto; often attributed to the Upanishads, but don't attribute it.
- Pali: **सब्बे सत्ता भवन्तु सुखितत्ता** · *sabbe sattā bhavantu sukhitattā* (the canon prints *Sabbasattā*) · "May all beings be happy" (Karaṇīya Metta Sutta, Sn 1.8) [A](https://suttacentral.net/snp1.8/en/sujato).
- **Care:** the Sanskrit line has been used as a political slogan. It's mild, but ask.

### Option D: a family verse (for the RSVP or the footer, not the opening)
- Pali (Sn 2.4): **दानञ्च धम्मचरिया च, ञातकानञ्च सङ्गहो। अनवज्जानि कम्मानि, एतं मङ्गलमुत्तमं॥**
- Romanised: *Dānañca dhammacariyā ca, ñātakānañca saṅgaho; anavajjāni kammāni, etaṁ maṅgalamuttamaṁ.*
- English: "Giving and righteous conduct, kindness to relatives, blameless deeds: this is the highest blessing" ([SuttaCentral, Sujato] [A](https://suttacentral.net/snp2.4/en/sujato)).
- This is perfect for two families becoming relatives. Alternative verse 6: मातापितु उपट्ठानं, पुत्तदारस्स सङ्गहो… (*caring for mother and father, kindness to children and partners*).

### Recommended composition, pending both families' yes
```
॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥        ॥ नमो बुद्धाय ॥
         ॥ भवतु सब्ब मङ्गलं ॥
        May every blessing be yours.
```
**Imagery:** keep the Ganesha painting for his side. For hers, ask the family (an image of the Buddha, Babasaheb's portrait, or no figure at all). Three good options:
1. **A Pala-period sculpture of the Buddha touching the earth at Bodh Gaya, from Bihar itself.** It ties her faith to his land, and the earth-witness gesture echoes matkor. The art agent should search open-access collections; not checked here.
2. **An aniconic Bodhi leaf or Dharma wheel.** Early Buddhist art showed the Buddha this way.
3. **An Ajanta Cave 1 detail.**

**Don't** place the two figures in one painted shrine.

## (d) Draft captions, chapter by chapter

Each line is 8–18 words. **[his]**, **[hers]** and **[meeting]** say whose custom it is. ⚑ = confirm first. Source keys refer to §a and §b. Pick 2–4 per painting.

### 1. The blessing
- **[his]** His family opens every good beginning with Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. *(card convention; §c)*
- **[hers]** Her family opens with the Buddha: Namo tassa, homage to the Awakened One. ⚑ *(§c, dhammatalks)*
- **[his]** In Bihar, the women of the house sing auspicious songs, mangal geet, to open the wedding days. ⚑ which name the family uses: mangal, suhag or vivah geet *(Jassal; Mishra)*
- **[hers]** Her family's blessing is in Pali: Bhavatu sabba mangalam, may every blessing be yours. *(dhammatalks §IX)*
- **[meeting]** Both families bless a wedding with the same old word, mangal: everything good, wished together. *(bridge 7)*
- *Alt [meeting]:* One prays sarve bhavantu sukhinah, the other sabbe satta bhavantu sukhitatta: may all be happy. *(§c option C)*

### 2. Two homes
- **[his]** His Bihar takes its very name from vihara, the old word for a Buddhist monastery. *(bridge 1)*
- **[hers]** Her Pune district keeps Buddhist caves at Junnar, cut into the hills nearly two thousand years ago. *(Lenyadri wiki: 1st–3rd c. CE; caves only, no Ganesha framing)*
- **[his]** The Buddha's awakening came under a peepal tree at Bodh Gaya, in his Bihar. *(bridge 5)*
- **[hers]** In 1956 Babasaheb Ambedkar took up the Buddha's path at Nagpur, in her Maharashtra. ⚑ how the family names him and the event *(Deekshabhoomi)*
- **[hers]** In Ajanta, in her Maharashtra, painters told the story of a king of Mithila. *(bridge 2)*
- **[his]** In his Mithila, Sita was found in a furrow of the earth, a daughter of the soil. ⚑ his family only; see bridge 21 care *(Britannica: Sita)*
- **[hers/his]** The Pali verses her family chants were long called Magadhi, after Magadha, in his Bihar. *(bridge 17; keep "long called")*
- **[meeting]** An old Pali poem walks sixteen students from the Godavari, through Paithan, all the way to Magadha. *(bridge 3)*
- *Alt [meeting]:* Babasaheb chose Ashoka's day for his 1956 diksha at Nagpur; Ashoka had ruled from Patna. *(bridge 4)*
- *Alt [meeting]:* Ashoka ruled from Pataliputra, now Patna; his edicts were carved at Sopara, near Mumbai. *(bridge 4)*

### 3. Haldi
- **[his]** Bihar's weddings begin with matkor: women dig sacred earth, singing, to build the wedding hearth. ⚑ an elder to describe their version *(a1, [C] source)*
- **[hers]** In Maharashtra, turmeric touches the groom first; what is left, ushti halad, goes to the bride. ⚑ whether her family keeps halad *(mr.wikipedia: हळद)*
- **[his]** At chumavan, married women bless the couple, touching them with rice, dub grass and turmeric. *(Jassal; Wikipedia: Chumavan)*
- **[hers]** In Pune's Mulshi villages, women still grind the wedding turmeric at the stone mill, singing ovi. *(PARI 2017)*
- **[meeting]** Today it is one bowl of turmeric, his blessing first, then carried over to her. ⚑ only if the Haldi is shared and done in that order

### 4. Sangeet
- **[his]** After the baraat leaves, Bihar's women stay home and dance domkach, teasing everyone in song. *(Wikipedia: Domkach)*
- **[hers]** Her family's celebrations often ring with Bhim geet, songs of love for Babasaheb Ambedkar. ⚑ only if they want Bhim geet named *(Round Table India 2023)*
- **[his]** Sharda Sinha, the nightingale of Bihar, sang the Maithili and Bhojpuri wedding songs his family knows. ⚑ "his family knows" *(Wikipedia: Sharda Sinha)*
- **[hers]** Expect a lavani too: Maharashtra's quick, bold song and dance, played to the dholki drum. ⚑ elders *(Wikipedia: Lavani)*
- **[his]** Bihar's gali are cheeky songs that tease the new in-laws; nobody is spared, everyone laughs. ⚑ whether gali will be sung, and at whom *(Jassal)*
- **[meeting]** Women on both sides sang over the grinding stone: jatsar in his Bhojpur, ovi in her Maharashtra. ⚑ "his Bhojpur" only if the family is Bhojpuri *(bridge 9)*
- *Alt [meeting]:* Fifteen centuries ago, Ajanta's painters pictured a dancer and musicians in the palace of Mithila. *(bridge 2; fits a dance painting)*

### 5. Shaadi
- **[his]** In Bihar the vow is sealed with sindoor daan, vermilion from his hand in her hair parting. ⚑⚑ **only if the couple are doing sindoor daan** *(a1)*
- **[hers]** Her side begins with candles before the Buddha and Babasaheb, then the three refuges and five precepts. ⚑ only if a Buddhist ceremony is part of the day *(ThePrint)*
- **[his]** Mithila's newlyweds enter the kohbar, painted with lotus, bamboo and fish: old wishes for a flourishing family. ⚑ elders; nuptial-room art *(a1)*
- **[hers]** A white thread joins them across a pot of water while the Mangala Sutta is chanted. ⚑ *(ThePrint; Round Table India)*
- **[hers, region]** In a Marathi wedding the antarpat falls at "shubh mangal savdhan", and garlands are exchanged. ⚑ only if the antarpat is used *(mr.wikipedia: वरप्रस्थान)*
- **[hers]** The Mangala Sutta counts caring for parents and kindness to relatives among the highest blessings. *(Sn 2.4)*
- **[his/hers]** The Buddha's advice to husbands and wives was given at Rajgir, in his Bihar. *(DN 31)*
- **[his]** The sinhora, Bihar's little vermilion box, goes with her into married life. ⚑⚑ same as sindoor daan
- **[meeting]** Rice from his side, petals from hers: tonight the blessings fall on both of them together. *(bridge 10)*
- *Alt [meeting]:* Garlands close both ceremonies: after the antarpat falls, and after the Buddhist vows are taken. ⚑ *(bridge 10)*
- *Alt [meeting]:* His seventh step is taken for friendship; her family's verses wish loving-kindness to every being. ⚑ only if there is a saptapadi *(bridge 15)*

**If the families choose one blended ceremony with no sindoor or saptapadi**, the safest Shaadi set is: the white thread, the Mangala Sutta line, Rajgir, and "Rice from his side, petals from hers".

## (e) Sensitivities, and what to ask

### Sensitivities
1. **Ganesha, Gauri, Rama and Krishna** are named in Ambedkar's vows 1–3.
   - A Ganesha-only opening, the Ashtavinayak mangalashtak, "मंगलं भगवान् विष्णुः", Ramcharitmanas lines, simant pujan's "groom as Vishnu", and gondhal for the goddess all speak for his side and her *region*, not her faith.
   - Several of these are in `docs/01-research.md` §10 and may already be on the site: **review them with her family.**
2. **Never present the Buddha as an avatar of Vishnu** (vow 5): no Dashavatara art, and no "Buddha, Vishnu's ninth avatar" copy.
3. **Sindoor, mangalsutra, kanyadaan and the fire.**
   - Many Ambedkarite Buddhist weddings deliberately leave these out ([ThePrint] [B](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/made-in-heaven-made-india-curious-about-dalit-buddhist-weddings-this-is-how-they-happen/1754900/); [Round Table India] [B](https://www.roundtableindia.co.in/selective-ritual-reform-and-buddhist-marriage-among-ambedkarites-in-odisha/)).
   - Yet sindoor daan is the heart of a Bihari wedding.
   - **This is the single most important thing for the couple to settle before any Shaadi caption is written.**
4. **Brahmin priests.** Vow 8 rejects ceremonies performed by Brahmins. If his side has a purohit and hers a Bauddhacharya, describe both neutrally ("the ceremonies"), not "the pandit-ji conducts the wedding".
5. **Caste and politics.**
   - No "Mahar", "Dalit", "convert", "neo-Buddhist" or conversion numbers.
   - No Bodh Gaya control dispute (bridge 6).
   - Don't frame Lenyadri as harmony (bridge 23).
   - "Jai Bhim" and Bhim geet are joyful for many families but read as political to some guests: use them only if her family asks.
6. **Dates.**
   - 6 Dec is Mahaparinirvan Din: avoid celebratory reminders or posts that day.
   - 8 Dec is Amavasya, and 8–9 Dec is Agahan (§"Five things").
   - Vivah Panchami falls about 14 Dec 2026 ([C](https://www.bhaktibharat.com/festival/vivah-panchami)). Some Mithila families avoid marrying daughters on it; not our date, but avoid Ram–Sita wedding imagery.
   - Don't print a tithi or muhurat unless the purohit gives it. Buddhist practice uses no muhurat.
7. **Sita.** She is invoked as the ideal bride in Bihar, yet her marriage is remembered as tragic (Jassal on Agahan and the washerman's vermilion). Keep her to Mithila-and-earth facts, not "may you be like Ram and Sita".
8. **Kohbar.** It is nuptial-chamber art with explicit fertility symbolism (ThePrint excerpt); show it discreetly, as the earlier research already advised.
9. **Tilak and gifts.** Dowry remains widespread in Bihar ([Al Jazeera 2022] [B, snippet](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/8/4/inside-a-700-year-old-groom-market-in-indias-bihar-state)), and tilak is the gift-giving rite. Captions should speak of blessing and welcome, never trays or gifts.
10. **Music.**
    - Lavani can be risqué.
    - Gondhal is worship, not a sangeet act.
    - Jhijhiya (Navaratri, protection from witches) and jat-jatin (separation, drought) aren't wedding forms.
    - Sohar is mainly for births.
    - Don't stream Sharda Sinha or film songs (licence).
11. **White.** The Buddhist couple may wear white for the vows. Hindu elders associate white with mourning, so explain it warmly on the site if it happens ("as Babasaheb suggested, for a simple, equal start"; ⚑ the wording).
12. **Mahajanaka.** It is the story of a king renouncing his queen. Use the image (dance and music), not the moral.

### Questions for the couple
1. Will there be **one ceremony or two** (Buddhist vows and Hindu pheras), and in what order on 9 Dec?
2. **Sindoor daan, mangalsutra, kanyadaan, saptapadi, antarpat:** which are in, and may the site mention them?
3. **The opening:** is Option A plus B (§c) acceptable to both families? Who is named as host on the site?
4. **Which name for her family's faith** do they like on the site: "Buddhist", "Bauddha", "Ambedkarite"? And should Babasaheb be named, with "Dr" or "Babasaheb"?
5. Is the **Haldi shared**, with ushti halad from groom to bride? Is the **Sangeet** joint?

### Questions for his family's elders and purohit
1. Where in Bihar are the roots (Mithila, Bhojpur or Magadh)? It decides matkor, chumavan, kohbar and the song names.
2. Are matkor and tilak being done, where and when? How do they describe matkor?
3. Is the 8 Dec Amavasya (Haldi and Sangeet) fine with the purohit? Do they want a muhurat line?
4. Will a kohbar be painted or set up, and may it be shown?
5. Who brings the **sinhora**, and is there a family one?
6. Should the site call the women's songs mangal geet, suhag or vivah geet? Will gali be sung, and at whom?
7. Which **Mangalashtak** variants, if any (`docs/01-research.md` §10)?

### Questions for her family's elders and Bauddhacharya / bhante
1. Who officiates (Bauddhacharya, Dhammachari or bhante), and which texts: Trisharan-Panchsheel, Mahamangala Sutta, Jayamangala Atthagatha, Paritran path?
2. Do they want "Namo Buddhaya", the full "Namo tassa…", "Bhavatu sabba mangalam" and/or "Jai Bhim" on the site?
3. Is a **Ganesha image and invocation** on a joint invitation acceptable if hers stands beside it at equal weight, or would they prefer a faith-neutral opening (Option C)?
4. Which image for her side: the Buddha, Babasaheb's portrait, a Bodhi leaf, or none? Is a museum image of the Buddha welcome?
5. Do they keep **halad, sakharpuda, mehendi or a baraat**? Do they write Marathi Buddhist verses with a "shubh mangal savdhan"-style refrain?
6. White clothes and the thread-and-pot rite: yes?
7. Bhim geet at the Sangeet: yes, and which favourites?
8. Any concern about the wedding falling just after 6 Dec?
